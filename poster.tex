  \begin{block}{Introduction \& Problem}

    Long-horizon robot manipulation requires two distinct capabilities: \textbf{motor skill} (how to execute an action) and \textbf{episodic memory} (what happened earlier in the episode). State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like $\pi_{0.5}$ handle the former well but lack the latter entirely.

    \vspace{0.5em}
    When tasks require recalling \textit{pre-occlusion state} --- such as which bin hid which colored cube before it was covered --- purely reactive policies fail catastrophically, even though the required motor primitives are identical to simpler variants.

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Why hasn't this been solved?} Prior work (MemER~\cite{memer}, MEM~\cite{mem}) addresses memory via \textit{imitation learning} on ${\sim}50$ demonstrations per task. We ask: can \textbf{reinforcement learning} do better, and which RL algorithm best preserves the structured output format required for grounded manipulation?

    \begin{itemize}
      \item Replay buffers grow unboundedly over long episodes.
      \item VLMs fine-tuned with sparse rewards risk \textit{format collapse} --- abandoning structured outputs for degenerate fixed responses.
      \item No large-scale demonstration datasets exist for memory-demanding manipulation tasks.
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    We propose a \textbf{hierarchical memory-augmented planner} above a frozen $\pi_{0.5}$ action expert. A Qwen3-VL-4B VLM maintains a keyframe memory buffer and emits grounded language subgoals, fine-tuned via a \textbf{quick algorithmic survey} of GRPO, RLOO, and PPO (with and without KL regularization) on dense task-completion reward.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Benchmark: RoboMME}

    We evaluate on \textbf{RoboMME}~\cite{robomme}, a suite of 16 ManiSkill tasks organized into four memory-demand categories:

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Counting} --- repeat actions a specified number of times (e.g., \textit{PickXtimes}, \textit{SwingXtimes}).
      \item \textbf{Persistent} --- recall and act on object state that has since been occluded (e.g., \textit{ButtonUnmask}, \textit{ButtonUnmaskSwap}).
      \item \textbf{Referential} --- resolve pronoun references to earlier-seen objects (e.g., \textit{VideoRepick}, \textit{VideoPlaceButton}).
      \item \textbf{Behavior} --- imitate a demonstrated behavior pattern (e.g., \textit{MoveCube}, \textit{PatternLock}).
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    RoboMME cleanly separates memory demand from motor difficulty: \textit{swap} and \textit{repick} variants use \textbf{identical motor primitives} to their base versions but require remembering pre-occlusion state. Success-rate differences are directly attributable to memory.

    \vspace{1em}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Approach}

    \textbf{Architecture.} Our system is a two-level hierarchy:

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{High-Level Planner (Qwen3-VL-4B + LoRA):} Receives the task instruction, a sliding window of recent frames, and a \textit{reveal-window keyframe buffer} (the memory tier). At each decision point it emits a grounded language subgoal with pixel coordinates.
      \item \textbf{Low-Level Actor ($\pi_{0.5}$, frozen):} Receives the current subgoal and raw observations; outputs continuous actions via its JAX/Flax diffusion policy.
    \end{enumerate}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Two-Tier Memory.}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textit{FIFO Frame Window}: last $N$ RGB frames, providing recent context.
      \item \textit{Keyframe Buffer}: heuristic reveal-window frames capturing pre-occlusion scene state (which bin hides which cube). Lucas's full GRPO branch extends this to \textit{learned} keyframe selection trained jointly with the subtask head.
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{1em}

    \end{figure}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{RL Survey: GRPO, RLOO, and PPO.}
    We compare four policy-gradient conditions for fine-tuning the planner, all from the same SFT warm-start:

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{GRPO}~\cite{grpo_2024}: samples $G{=}4$ rollouts per prompt, uses group-relative advantage $\hat{A}_k = r_k - \bar{r}$, no value network.
      \item \textbf{RLOO}: leave-one-out baseline $\hat{A}_k = r_k - \tfrac{\sum_{j \neq k} r_j}{K-1}$; unbiased, no value network, no grouping overhead.
      \item \textbf{PPO ($\beta{=}0$)}: scalar value head $V(s)$ trained alongside LoRA; advantage $\hat{A}_k = r_k - V(s).\texttt{detach}()$. Contextual-bandit variant (no GAE, single on-policy update). No KL anchor.
      \item \textbf{PPO ($\beta{=}0.1$)}: same as above with quadratic KL penalty to SFT reference, preventing the policy from drifting too far from the grounded output format.
    \end{itemize}

    All conditions use \textbf{LoRA} adapters on Qwen3-VL-4B, HuggingFace \texttt{generate()} with $K$ parallel sequences, and a \textbf{dense task-completion fraction} $r \in [0,1]$ from ManiSkill's sequential-task tracker as the reward signal (not binary success).

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Infrastructure.} JAX/Flax $\pi_{0.5}$ policy server and ManiSkill (CPU/OSMesa rendering) co-located in one Modal A100-80GB container, communicating over a localhost WebSocket. SFT warm-start (5 epochs, lr $4 \times 10^{-6}$) precedes all RL conditions.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Experimental Results}

    \textbf{RL algorithmic survey} on ButtonUnmask val (50 ep, seed 0).
    All RL conditions fine-tune from the same SFT checkpoint.

    \begin{center}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Method} & \textbf{Mean Reward$^\dagger$} & \textbf{Subgoal Format} \\
      \midrule
      Zero-shot Qwen3-VL-4B           & 0.30 & Simple, no coords \\
      + SFT warm-start (IL only)      & 0.30 & Grounded $\langle x,y\rangle$ $\checkmark$ \\
      + PPO, $\beta_\text{KL}{=}0$    & 0.27 & \textbf{Collapsed} $\times$ \\
      + PPO, $\beta_\text{KL}{=}0.1$  & 0.28 & Grounded $\langle x,y\rangle$ $\checkmark$ \\
      + GRPO / RLOO (any $K$)          & 0.30 & Grounded — 0 grad steps$^*$ \\
      \midrule
      \textit{Target:} MemER~\cite{memer} & \textit{---} & \textit{72\% SR on ButtonUnmask} \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \vspace{0.3em}
    \footnotesize{$^\dagger$Dense task-completion fraction $r\!\in\![0,1]$; not directly comparable to MemER success rate.\\
    $^*$Coordinate space mismatch causes constant subgoal output --- see below.}

    \vspace{0.8em}
    \textbf{Key finding 1 — Format collapse.}
    PPO without KL ($\beta{=}0$) abandons the grounded $\langle x,y\rangle$ format entirely, outputting a fixed phrase regardless of scene. KL anchoring ($\beta{=}0.1$) preserves format.

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \textbf{Key finding 2 — Coordinate space bottleneck.}
    All methods plateau at mean reward ${\approx}0.30$ --- identical to zero-shot. Root cause: SFT targets used raw pixel coordinates ($\langle y,x\rangle$, 0--256) while Qwen3-VL grounds natively in normalized space ($\langle x,y\rangle$, 0--1000). The model learned a constant near-center coordinate regardless of scene, eliminating reward variance and making group-based RL (GRPO, RLOO) degenerate (zero gradient steps at any $K$). Lucas's \texttt{to\_qwen\_xy}/\texttt{from\_qwen\_xy} fix addresses this directly.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Ablations}

    To isolate each component's contribution:

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{SFT only} --- IL warm-start, no RL (replicates MemER/MEM~\cite{memer,mem}).
      \item \textbf{GRPO} --- group-relative baseline, $G{=}4$, $\beta{=}0$.
      \item \textbf{RLOO} --- leave-one-out baseline, same $K$ rollouts as GRPO, no value head.
      \item \textbf{PPO $\beta{=}0$} --- learned value baseline, no KL anchor.
      \item \textbf{PPO $\beta{=}0.1$} --- value baseline + KL constraint to SFT reference.
    \end{enumerate}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    All five conditions share SFT checkpoint, task (ButtonUnmask), rollout budget ($K{=}4$, max 100 steps), and training steps (25). This controls for compute and isolates the algorithmic variable.

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Lucas's full GRPO contribution} (in progress): streaming memory across multiple picks per episode, jointly trained keyframe selection, ButtonUnmaskSwap (harder Permanence variant), and corrected Qwen-VL coordinate space ($\langle x,y \rangle$ 0--1000). This constitutes the primary research claim of the paper.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Conclusions, Limitations \& Future Work}

    \textbf{Conclusions.} We present a hierarchical memory-augmented VLA and identify two failure modes blocking RL-based memory training: (1) \textbf{format collapse} --- RL without KL regularization destroys the grounded coordinate output format; (2) \textbf{coordinate space mismatch} --- SFT in the wrong pixel space collapses subgoal diversity, making group-based RL (GRPO, RLOO) degenerate. Together these explain why our RL survey plateaus at the same level as zero-shot Qwen (mean reward 0.30), far below MemER-IL (72\% on ButtonUnmask). Lucas's coordinate fix and streaming GRPO are the proposed solutions.

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Limitations.}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Format collapse under RL}: PPO without KL destroys the grounded coordinate format. Mitigation: KL anchoring; or lower learning rate during RL phase.
      \item \textbf{Short training budget}: 25-step survey is sufficient for algorithmic comparison but insufficient for convergence. Full-scale runs require 200+ steps (${\sim}$17 hours on A100 at current rollout speed).
      \item \textbf{Rollout bottleneck}: ManiSkill CPU rendering dominates wall time (${\sim}5$ min/step). GPU rendering or parallelized environments would accelerate training significantly.
      \item Binary reward is sparse for the hardest tasks; mitigation: ManiSkill progress shaping or a base-to-swap curriculum.
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Future Work.} Full-scale GRPO with streaming keyframe selection on ButtonUnmaskSwap; extend to multi-object tracking tasks; GPU-accelerated rollout environments; generalization to out-of-distribution RoboCasa environments.

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Takeaway.} Hierarchical decomposition --- separating \textit{what to do} (VLM planner with memory) from \textit{how to do it} (frozen VLA actor) --- is a tractable path to long-horizon manipulation. However, RL fine-tuning of the planner requires careful regularization to preserve structured grounded outputs.

  \end{block}
